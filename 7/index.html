The Eight Golden Rules of Interface Design

As defined by Ben Shneiderman

  • 1. Strive for consistency
  • 2. Seek universal usability
  • 3. Offer informative feedback
  • 4. Design dialogs to yield closure
  • 5. Prevent errors
  • 6. Permit easy reversal of actions
  • 7. Keep users in control
  • 8. Reduce short-term memory load

7. Keep users in control

Responsive feedback to interactions is very important to the user experience. Users like to feel in control, especially experienced users. They want to be the driving force and they don't want the system to make decisions on their behalf. It's why we aren't doing things automatically on this website. You choose where to go and what to interact with. Users also don't like unexplained changes. People get used to a process and may be irritated if it suddenly changes.
