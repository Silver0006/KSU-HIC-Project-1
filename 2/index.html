The Eight Golden Rules of Interface Design

As defined by Ben Shneiderman

  • 1. Strive for consistency
  • 2. Seek universal usability
  • 3. Offer informative feedback
  • 4. Design dialogs to yield closure
  • 5. Prevent errors
  • 6. Permit easy reversal of actions
  • 7. Keep users in control
  • 8. Reduce short-term memory load

2. Seek universal usability

An interface should work for everyone: beginners and experts, young and old, and people with different abilities or devices.

So in this page you are able to zoom in and out of this page. This is mainly to help people who find it better to have letters bigger such as older people.

Also implemented is a shortcut of just pressing + and - to zoom in and out. This follows the 2nd golden rule which is having an interface that helps everyone experienced or not, along with having shortcuts.

Examples such as:

  • Adjustable text size for people with low vision
  • High contrast colors for people who struggle to see light text
  • Keyboard shortcuts for experts, simple menus for beginners
  • Pages that work on phones as well as computers
