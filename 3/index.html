The Eight Golden Rules of Interface Design

As defined by Ben Shneiderman

  • 1. Strive for consistency
  • 2. Seek universal usability
  • 3. Offer informative feedback
  • 4. Design dialogs to yield closure
  • 5. Prevent errors
  • 6. Permit easy reversal of actions
  • 7. Keep users in control
  • 8. Reduce short-term memory load

3. Offer informative feedback

Have you ever received a error from a program or website? If you've used a computer, it is likely. Error are designed to inform. And the more they inform the better. When designing for user experience, you want an error to be easy to understand. Providing no context can be a quick way to annoy users. Even worse, what if the program doesn't inform you of the error. For example, if you are submitting a document and it fails. Depending on the document, it could be incredibly harmful to think you submitted something when you did not. For example, any financial information or transaction. Providing descriptive error messages not only benefits your users, but it can help reduce negative impression by providing users with reasonable explanations of why something failed.

Okay, why not just provide the direct error to the user. Well, not every user is going to have the knowledge to understand. Another great feedback method is to provide a course of action. Providing users with a link to get help or a suggesting to wait and try again are both good methods. But remember a suggestion does not take the place of being descriptive.

Take a look at the examples below to get a better understanding.

Bad examples

  • Error
    • - No description.
  • Error: code 404
    • - Not everyone knows error codes.
  • An unexpected error has occurred.
    • - Yep most errors are unexpected, what & why???
  • Error: please try again later.
    • - When is later?
    - What did the error happened?

Good examples

  • Upload failed - Unable to establish connection. Please try again. If the issue continues try contacting bjim@breweremailemporium.com
    • - Explains the issue is that the upload failed due to a lack of connection.
    - Provides a clear course of action.
    - Provides a method of contact is the issue continues.
  • Error: Invalid date. Format is year-month-date. Ex: 2001-01-01
    • - Error is from an invalid date.
    - Explains the issue is the formatting of the date.
    - Provides a example showing the correct way to format the date.
